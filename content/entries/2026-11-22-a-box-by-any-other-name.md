In a [June 2025 post](/2025/06/22/sleep-the-final-boss-of-recovery/), after a fairly exhausting account of sleep and work, I introduced two names: RestoreBox for my shipping packaging, and RestorePort Soniclean for an ultrasonic cleaning service.

RestorePort Soniclean. Somewhere between a repair service and a dental appointment.

I immediately admitted I wasn't entirely sold on the name. Then I added that it did what it needed to do for now, just like me.

Looking back at that sentence, the useful part is the separation it almost makes. Being satisfied with a name and being satisfied with the thing underneath it are two different decisions. A box has a job. A cleaning service has a job. The name has the considerably smaller job of helping somebody recognize what is being offered.

That old post doesn't establish how well either offering eventually worked. It described a website approaching launch, with some services still to come. I can't award myself a satisfied customer on the strength of a product name, however many capital letters I managed to squeeze into it.

But the uncertainty about the wording was already public. I had named something without declaring the name a permanent triumph. That is a surprisingly useful little patch of honesty in an announcement: here is what I am trying to offer, and here is a part I may still change.

There is a limit to that permission. “For now” can reasonably apply to a label without applying to everything the label promises. If somebody sends a phone for repair, the provisional part shouldn't be whether they get their phone back. A name can remain under consideration while the obligation it describes needs to be quite clear. That is a distinction worth drawing from the old announcement, not a claim about any particular transaction.

The joke about doing what was needed for now put me beside the name, both apparently awaiting further refinement. I wouldn't turn that into a declaration that people are products. It does leave a good question, though: which parts actually need to be dependable, and which parts am I merely insisting must sound finished?

Those are different kinds of unfinished. Confusing them gives a name too much authority over the work, or gives unfinished work too much shelter behind a name.

RestoreBox is pleasantly direct. RestorePort Soniclean is still a mouthful in the historical record. Neither needs to carry the whole weight of whether its inventor had his life sorted out.

A box should be allowed to remain a box. Even if the man naming it is still a work in progress.
