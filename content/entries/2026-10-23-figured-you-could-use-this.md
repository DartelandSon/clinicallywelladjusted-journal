In December 2024, I introduced a business I was building from my kitchen table. By June 2025, people were bringing me old consoles, routers, and office hardware. The explanation, as I recorded it then, was “figured you could use this.”

There are grander endorsements. Nobody was offering to put my face on currency. But there is something wonderfully specific about being trusted with a thing somebody else no longer knows what to do with. A business plan describes what might happen. An old router is considerably less eloquent. It still requires an answer.

In that [June account](/2025/06/22/changing-meds-again/), repairs had spread from friends to friends of friends, then to people I didn’t really know. I estimated the repairable or resellable gear at about $2,000. That was my estimate at the time, not a record of sales, profit, or a sudden escape from financial trouble. The distinction matters. A pile of possibilities does not pay a bill merely by being a pile.

It does, however, make an interesting contrast with the beginning of the story. In my [first post](/2024/12/15/solving-e-polution-one-repair-at-a-time-because-sanity-is-overrated/), I was trying to explain how a person on disability income could build a repair business. Six months later, the work was beginning to arrive through people who knew somebody who knew me. The plan had acquired objects. Some assembly, diagnosis, and a less romantic amount of actual work still required.

Looking back at those two accounts, there is a smaller story underneath the business story: usefulness passing from one person to another. Someone had equipment they no longer needed. I had repair skills and a business taking shape. That is all the record establishes. I don’t know what every person intended, and I don’t need to appoint an old console the ambassador of somebody else’s faith in me.

Nor does the archive tell me what became of every machine. I can’t turn the whole collection into a success story just because that would make a tidier ending. Some of the most honest words in the original post were the admission that I wasn’t sure where it was heading.

The uncertainty belongs in the story alongside the gear. Before there was an answer about where the business would go, there were people bringing things over because they thought I could use them. Not a finished enterprise. Not a verdict on the rest of my life. An old router, and a reason to see what could be done with it.
